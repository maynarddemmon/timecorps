BORNE, Arthur Caldwell — Deep Placement Agent, Time Corps (ID: AB)

Borne was born in 2131 in the Glasgow Canal District and trained as a marine engineer before the Corps ever heard of him. He spent a decade maintaining the tidal barriers of the Clyde, and he still talks about water pressure the way other agents talk about paradox. The Corps recruited him at thirty for exactly that knowledge: the maritime events of the early twentieth century were becoming a priority, and it needed agents who could pass as working engineers without a script.

Deep Placement suits him. He has completed four long insertions, the longest lasting eleven months as a shipyard foreman in 1908. He is patient, unhurried and entirely at home in the grime of an industrial period, and his handlers say he is the only agent they have ever had to remind to come home. The small paradox load on his file dates from that long placement, when a delayed recall forced him to pass through an event he had already visited.

Borne keeps a period habit he picked up during his first placement and has never tried to break: he smokes, always the brand of whatever decade he is in. The medical wing disapproves. He points out that nothing identifies an outsider faster than a man who does not know what to do with his hands.

He is gruff, practical and generous with junior agents, who seek him out for advice on surviving long placements. His one firm rule, repeated to every one of them, is that you must never start to think of the past as home, however long you stay there.
