HALLOWAY, Thomas Arden — Deep Placement Agent, Time Corps (ID: HW)

Halloway was born in 2144 in the Sheffield Corridor, the son of structural engineers who rebuilt the northern floodworks. He was drafting load calculations by twelve and designing bridges by sixteen, and he entered the Nexus academy through its engineering track with no intention of ever working in the field. His instructors thought otherwise. His ability to pass as a period engineer, speaking fluently about rivets, tonnage and steam pressure in any decade from 1850 onward, made him a natural candidate for Deep Placement.

Deep Placement agents do not jump in and out of events. They live inside them, sometimes for months, building the ordinary relationships that let a small intervention land naturally. Halloway was inserted into the Harland & Wolff drafting offices in 1911 under the name T. A. Halloway, junior naval architect, with orders to observe the Olympic-class design reviews and report on the lifeboat davit decisions. His early reports were meticulous, and his quarterly contact checks arrived on schedule.

In the autumn of 1911 his reports stopped. Command's best explanation is a combination of paradox accumulation from the long placement and a deliberate decision on Halloway's part to go silent rather than risk exposure. Corps doctrine forbids extraction without contact, so he was listed as unaccounted for, not lost. His file carries a standing instruction: any agent attesting the Harland & Wolff events of early 1912 should expect to find him there.

Halloway is quiet, patient and almost impossible to hurry. Agents who trained with him remember that he would redraw a design three times rather than submit one he was not certain of. If he is recovered, Command expects him to carry a significant paradox load, and it will not allow him to extend his placement further without a full medical review.
