VASQUEZ, Rafael Ignacio — Field Agent, Time Corps

Vasquez was born in 2139 in the Lima Reclamation Zone, one of the coastal settlements rebuilt after the Pacific Retreat. He grew up among salvage crews pulling pre-Retreat archives out of flooded municipal vaults, and by fifteen he could read water-damaged paper records faster than most trained archivists. That childhood left him with a lasting conviction that the past is not fixed history but fragile evidence, easily lost and easily misread. A Corps recruiter noticed his reconstruction of a drowned city registry in 2158. He was the youngest candidate admitted to the Nexus academy that year.

His training record is uneven in ways the instructors noted with interest rather than concern. He consistently scored below average on chronal efficiency, spending more jump energy than the models predicted. He also scored in the top percentile for attestation: once on site, he confirms what actually happened faster and more completely than nearly any agent of his cohort. Evaluators described him as "slow to arrive and impossible to fool." His final assessment carries a single handwritten remark from the academy director: Trust his reads. Watch his reserves.

The scar across his left brow and cheek comes from his third field deployment, the Tunguska containment of 2166. A cascading paradox at that site drew a Chronovore manifestation, and Vasquez held the event boundary long enough for two junior agents to recall to HQ before he withdrew himself. He came back with the scar, a formal commendation, and a paradox load that kept him off the active roster for fourteen months. He refused reassignment to analysis work and requalified for field duty on his first attempt.

Colleagues describe Vasquez as reserved, exacting, and fiercely protective of the agents deployed beside him. He is openly skeptical of interventions that treat historical events as puzzles to be solved rather than lives to be preserved, and his mission reports tend to record casualty figures before anything else. He keeps a handwritten ledger of every event he has altered, listing both the lives changed and the paradox incurred. He has declined three offers to train new recruits, saying that he has not yet earned the right to tell anyone else how it should be done.
