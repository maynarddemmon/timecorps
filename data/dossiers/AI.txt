INGRAM, Aldous Percival — Field Historian, Archive Division (ID: AI)

Ingram was born in 2128 in the Oxford Archive Enclave, one of the few institutions that preserved its library intact through the Retreat. He was a scholar long before he was an agent, a specialist in the social history of the early industrial world with two published monographs on Victorian letter-writing. The Corps recruited him at thirty-five to advise on period behavior, and he talked his way into field certification within two years.

He is the Corps' leading expert on the manners, speech and correspondence of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. He can date a letter by its salutation, tell a Belfast shipwright from a Liverpool one by a single phrase, and reconstruct a conversation from the replies that survive. His attestation work is excellent. His jump efficiency is poor, and he has never been sent on a mission where speed mattered.

Ingram dresses in the manner of whatever period he last visited, with a fondness for gloves that his colleagues tease him about. He says it is practical: gloved hands leave no fingerprints and draw no attention, and in most of the periods he visits a bare-handed gentleman would be remarked upon.

He is thoughtful, courteous and slow to reach conclusions, and he dislikes being hurried in the field or anywhere else. Younger agents consult him before difficult social insertions, and he answers every question in more detail than they asked for and, usually, exactly as much as they needed.
