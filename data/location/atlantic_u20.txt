U-20 — Imperial German Navy, off the south coast of Ireland

U-20 is a diesel-electric submarine of the German navy, commissioned in 1913, with a crew of about 35. On the surface she can make around 15 knots; submerged, on her batteries, little more than 9, and not for long. She carries a small supply of torpedoes for her four tubes.

Her commander is Kapitänleutnant Walther Schwieger, thirty years old, regarded as one of the navy's most capable submarine officers. U-20 left Emden on 30 April for a patrol around the north of Scotland to the waters off Ireland and the approaches to Liverpool. Germany has declared the seas around Britain a war zone, and British merchant ships in it liable to be sunk.

By 7 May she has sunk a sailing ship and two steamers, run into repeated fog, and used most of her torpedoes. Schwieger is thinking of the long voyage home.

OPERATIVE NOTES: A submarine at sea is beyond reach: there is no way aboard, and nowhere to hide once there. What can be changed is what she finds. A target that zigzags, or keeps well offshore, or runs at full speed, may never come within her range.
