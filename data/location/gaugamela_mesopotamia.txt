UPPER MESOPOTAMIA — Between the Tigris and the Zagros, Assyria
<p>Rolling, open country east of the Tigris, near the ruins of Nineveh and the town of Arbela, on the road from Asia Minor to Babylon. The plain at Gaugamela, “the Camel’s House”, is wide and flat.
<p>Darius has had it leveled further, so his scythed chariots can charge and his great army can use its numbers. His army draws on every people of the empire, from Bactrians and Scythians to Indians with fifteen elephants.
<p>Alexander’s army is smaller, but it has the Macedonian phalanx, with its eighteen-foot spears, and the Companion cavalry, which Alexander leads himself.
<p>OPERATIVE NOTES: Both armies are full of foreigners. Greek mercenaries fight for Darius, and Persians and Asians now serve Alexander. Camp followers, traders and scouts move between the lines.
