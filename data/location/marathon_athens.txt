ATHENS — Attica, Greece
<p>A city of perhaps thirty thousand citizens, crowded around the rock of the Acropolis, with its old temple of Athena on top. The walls are old and not strong. Phaleron, the open bay that serves as its harbor, lies about three miles to the southwest.
<p>Athens has governed itself as a democracy for only eighteen years, since the reforms of Cleisthenes. The family of Hippias, who ruled the city as tyrants until twenty years ago, still has friends inside the walls, and so does Persia.
<p>The army is the citizens themselves. When it marches out to Marathon, the city is left to the old, the young, the women and the slaves.
<p>OPERATIVE NOTES: Every citizen is known by name, father and deme. A stranger will be taken for a foreigner, which is common enough in a trading city, but foreigners have no say in the assembly and are watched closely in a crisis.
