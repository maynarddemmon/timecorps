RMS LUSITANIA — Cunard Line, New York to Liverpool

When she entered service in 1907 the Lusitania was the largest and fastest ship in the world. Built by John Brown on the Clyde with a government loan and subsidy, she was designed from the start to be requisitioned in wartime, and her first Atlantic crossings won back the Blue Riband for Britain.

She is 787 feet long and measures more than 31,000 gross tons. Steam turbines turn her four propellers, and at full power she can make 25 knots. Her four tall funnels made her one of the most recognizable ships on the ocean, and that fame makes her a prize.

In May 1915 the Admiralty has not taken her into service, and she runs a reduced wartime schedule under Captain William Turner, a veteran Cunard master who knows her well. War has thinned her passenger lists and her crew, and closed one of her boiler rooms.

OPERATIVE NOTES: Cunard's wartime crew is a mix of old hands and new men signed on to replace those called up, and new faces are common. First-class passengers have the run of the upper decks; third class is kept below and apart.
