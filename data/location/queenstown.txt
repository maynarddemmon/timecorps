QUEENSTOWN — County Cork, Ireland

Queenstown, now known as Cobh, is the port town of Cork Harbour, one of the largest natural harbours in the world. For generations it has been the last port of call for transatlantic liners and the last glimpse of Ireland for many thousands of emigrants. On 11 April 1912 the Titanic anchored off Queenstown for the last time, taking on passengers from the tenders before she sailed for New York.

In wartime it is also a naval base, headquarters of the admiral commanding the coast of Ireland. The harbour holds tugs, tenders, trawlers, naval auxiliaries and fishing boats, all of which can be pressed into rescue work.

After a sinking off the Old Head of Kinsale, Queenstown is where survivors and the dead are brought. Its hotels, homes and public buildings become hospitals and mortuaries, and the dead who are not claimed are buried in mass graves at the Old Church Cemetery above the town.

OPERATIVE NOTES: Rescue craft need time to raise steam before they can sail, and orders to send them come from the naval headquarters ashore. A harbour ready in advance can reach the scene hours sooner than one that must wait for the news.
