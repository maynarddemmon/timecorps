SARDIS — Lydia, Asia Minor
<p>The old capital of King Croesus, on a spur of Mount Tmolus above the plain of the Hermus River, now the seat of the Persian satrap of Lydia. The Royal Road runs from here to Susa, some 1,600 miles, with a post station every day’s ride.
<p>The Ionian Greeks burned the lower city in 498 BCE, with Athenian help. That is the reason the Persians came to Marathon, and back again.
<p>Gold washed down from Tmolus by the Pactolus River made Lydia rich, and the first coins were struck here.
<p>OPERATIVE NOTES: Sardis is full of Greeks: merchants, mercenaries, exiles and spies. A Greek is unremarkable here, but the satrap’s court is guarded and suspicious, and an audience takes gifts.
