CROW'S NEST — Foremast, RMS Titanic

The crow's nest is a small open cage fixed to the foremast, reached by an iron ladder that climbs up inside the hollow mast from a door on the forward well deck. It stands roughly ninety feet above the sea. It has a bell for signalling what is seen, and a telephone to the bridge.

Six lookouts share the duty in pairs, standing two-hour watches. On the night of 14 April the watch from 22:00 to midnight belongs to Frederick Fleet and Reginald Lee. There is no shelter up here, and the cold of a clear night at sea cuts through any coat.

A locker in the nest is meant to hold binoculars for the lookouts. The lookouts had them on the voyage from Belfast. Since Southampton the locker has stayed shut, and its key has not been found.

OPERATIVE NOTES: The ladder is inside the mast and hard to climb unseen. The lookouts change watch on the hour, and the relieving pair passes on its orders at the top. Anyone else in the nest would be noticed at once, and reported.
