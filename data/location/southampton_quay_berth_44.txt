BERTH 44 — White Star Dock, Southampton

White Star moved its express service from Liverpool to Southampton in 1907, and the dock was deepened and lengthened so that ships of the Olympic class could berth here. Berth 44 is where the Titanic lies from her arrival from Belfast in the early hours of 4 April until she sails at noon on the 10th.

The quay is crowded day and night. Coal comes aboard by the ton; a national coal strike has only just ended, and the Titanic's bunkers are being filled partly with coal taken from other ships of the line, left idle at their moorings. Stores, linen, crockery and mail go up the gangways in an endless stream. Most of her crew are Southampton men, signed on at the last minute from the streets of Northam and Chapel a few minutes' walk away.

On sailing day, boat trains from Waterloo run straight onto the quay beside the ship, and passengers step from their carriages to the gangways.

OPERATIVE NOTES: Crew signing-on is chaotic, and a seaman's discharge book will get a man aboard with few questions. Officers' appointments are made by White Star's marine superintendent, not by the ship's master; changes to who sails are decided ashore.
