PIER 54 — Cunard Line, North River, New York

Pier 54 stands at the foot of West Fourteenth Street on the Hudson, part of the long row of new Chelsea Piers built to take the great Atlantic liners. It is Cunard's New York terminal. In April 1912 the Carpathia brought the Titanic's survivors to this same pier, landing them in the rain before a silent crowd of thousands.

In the spring of 1915 the Lusitania sails from here on her regular run to Liverpool. The pier shed is crowded with longshoremen, passengers, well-wishers and, on 1 May, reporters asking passengers whether they have seen the German Embassy's warning in the morning papers.

Cargo is loaded into her holds from the pier in the days before sailing. The United States is neutral, but American firms may legally sell munitions to the warring powers, and they ship them in British liners.

OPERATIVE NOTES: The pier is busy, public and loosely watched; a longshoreman's clothes or a passenger's ticket will get you onto it. Cargo manifests are handled by Cunard's pier office. Customs clearance for the supplementary manifest will be filed only after the ship sails.
