STRAITS OF SALAMIS — Saronic Gulf, Greece
<p>A narrow channel, about a mile wide at its narrowest, between the island of Salamis and the coast of Attica, west of Athens and its harbor at Phaleron. The small island of Psyttaleia sits at its eastern mouth.
<p>In September of 480 BCE, Salamis is crowded with refugees from Athens, and the Greek fleet lies along its eastern shore. Across the water, the Persians have taken Athens and burned the Acropolis.
<p>A trireme is a long, light warship rowed by 170 men in three banks, with a bronze ram at the bow. In open water, the side with more ships wins. In the narrows, numbers count for less, and weight and discipline for more.
<p>OPERATIVE NOTES: Each city’s ships keep to themselves, and crews know their own officers. Boats pass between the fleets at night, and a Greek speaker with a good story can cross to the Persian side; Ionian Greeks row for both.
