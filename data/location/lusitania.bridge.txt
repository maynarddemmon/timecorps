BRIDGE — RMS Lusitania

The Lusitania's bridge spans the front of her boat deck, with the wheelhouse, chart room and captain's quarters close behind it. On the morning of 7 May it is under the command of Captain William Turner, a blunt, experienced master who has sailed the Atlantic most of his life and does not much like being told how to do it.

The Admiralty's confidential advice has reached the bridge in several forms: keep away from headlands, steer a mid-channel course, zigzag in waters where submarines have been seen, keep up speed. Turner reads it in the light of long habit. Having run through fog for part of the morning, he wants an exact fix before making for the Mersey.

A four-point bearing gives that fix. The officer takes a bearing on a landmark when it lies four points, or 45 degrees, off the bow, and again when it lies abeam. The distance run between the two is the ship's distance off the land. It requires holding one steady course and speed for the whole time.

OPERATIVE NOTES: The bridge officers are loyal to Turner and wary of interference. An order to change course will be obeyed only if it comes from the captain himself, or reaches him in a form he trusts, such as a direct Admiralty instruction.
