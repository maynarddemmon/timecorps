SS CALIFORNIAN — Leyland Line, North Atlantic

The Californian is a Leyland Line cargo steamer of just over 6,000 tons, bound from London to Boston with no passengers aboard this voyage. Her master is Captain Stanley Lord, a capable and experienced officer.

On the evening of 14 April she runs into field ice, and at 22:21 Lord stops his ship for the night rather than push on through it in the dark. She lies stopped, drifting slowly in the ice, somewhere to the north of the Titanic's position.

She carries one wireless operator, Cyril Evans, who works his set through the evening and then, like the rest of the ship's company, goes to bed. When his headphones come off, the Californian is deaf. What her officers see on watch that night, they will have to interpret without him.

OPERATIVE NOTES: A small crew in a stopped ship at night: the officer of the watch on the bridge, an apprentice or two, and everyone else asleep. The wireless cabin is unattended once Evans turns in. Waking the captain, or the operator, is a matter for the officer of the watch.
