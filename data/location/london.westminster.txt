WESTMINSTER — London

Westminster is the seat of British government: Parliament, the great ministries along Whitehall, and the halls and hotels where official business spills over. In the winter of 1913 to 1914 the International Conference on Safety of Life at Sea meets here, and on 20 January 1914 the delegates sign its convention.

A year and a half later, with the country at war, Westminster hosts the Board of Trade's formal investigation into the loss of the Lusitania. It is presided over by Lord Mersey, the same judge who led the British inquiry into the Titanic. The public hearings open at the Central Hall, Westminster, on 15 June 1915. The most sensitive evidence, touching on the Admiralty's warnings and instructions, is heard in closed session.

The inquiry's findings will decide, in the public mind, who was responsible for the disaster: the German government, the Admiralty, or the Lusitania's captain.

OPERATIVE NOTES: The public sessions are open to anyone with a seat; the closed sessions are not. Counsel, clerks and witnesses come and go constantly. Documents entered into the record are copied and preserved; documents that never reach the hearing are simply lost.
