FORWARD HOLDS — RMS Lusitania

The Lusitania's forward cargo holds lie beneath the forecastle and the forward decks, ahead of the first boiler room. On the May 1915 crossing they carry a typical cargo for the Liverpool run: copper, brass, machinery, furs, cheese, foodstuffs and bales of general goods, together with sacks of mail.

They also carry some 4,200 cases of rifle cartridges and around 1,250 cases of shrapnel shells, listed on a supplementary manifest filed after she sails. Shipping them is legal under American law, and it is not the first time she has carried such a cargo.

The holds are reached by hatches from the forward deck and are closed and battened down for the crossing.

OPERATIVE NOTES: Cargo is loaded at Pier 54 in the days before sailing and checked against the pier office's lists. Once the hatches are closed for sea, there is no practical way to move cargo in or out. Anything to be changed must be changed on the pier.
