PLAIN OF MARATHON — Northeast Attica, Greece
<p>A crescent of flat ground about six miles long, between the mountains and a shallow bay on the east coast of Attica, some twenty-five miles from Athens by the coast road. Marsh closes the plain at its northern end, and a smaller marsh lies to the south. The beach shelves gently, so a fleet can run its ships ashore along the whole length of the bay.
<p>The plain is good ground for horses, which is why Hippias, the exiled tyrant of Athens, has led the Persians here. He landed at Marathon with his father Peisistratos half a century ago, on the way to taking the city.
<p>The Athenian army camps on the higher ground at the southern end of the plain, in a sanctuary of Herakles, where it blocks the road to Athens. A mile of open ground lies between the two armies.
<p>OPERATIVE NOTES: Both camps are full of foreigners. The Persian army draws men from across the empire, Ionian Greeks among them, so a Greek speaker can pass in the Persian camp if no one looks closely. In the Athenian camp, everyone knows his neighbors by tribe and deme; a stranger there will be questioned.
