THE ADMIRALTY — Whitehall, London

The Admiralty directs the Royal Navy from its buildings on Whitehall, beside Horse Guards Parade. In May 1915 its political head, the First Lord, is Winston Churchill, away in Paris during the days that matter; its professional head, the First Sea Lord, is the aged and exhausted Admiral Lord Fisher.

The Admiralty is responsible for protecting British shipping in home waters, and for advising merchant masters how to avoid submarines. Its warnings go out by wireless in general terms. Its advice, issued in confidential instructions to masters, is to avoid headlands, steer mid-channel, zigzag, and keep up speed.

Inside the Old Admiralty Building, the codebreakers of Room 40 read the German navy's wireless signals. They know U-20 is at sea and roughly where she is operating. That knowledge is guarded jealously, shared with very few, and almost never passed on to the ships at sea.

OPERATIVE NOTES: The building is full of officers and civil servants who do not all know one another; a naval uniform or a clerk's frock coat will pass in the corridors. Room 40 does not. Its door is guarded, and its staff are a small, closed circle.
