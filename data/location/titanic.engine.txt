ENGINE ROOMS — Aft, RMS Titanic

Deep in the ship, abaft the boiler rooms, the reciprocating engine room holds two enormous four-cylinder, triple-expansion engines, each taller than a house. Behind them, the turbine room houses the low-pressure turbine that drives the centre propeller. The turbine can only turn ahead; when the engines are reversed, it stops.

Forward of the engines lie six boiler rooms and twenty-nine boilers fed by 159 furnaces. The firemen feed them by hand, the trimmers wheel coal from the bunkers, and together they burn some six hundred tons a day. The heat is ferocious and the noise constant.

Chief Engineer Joseph Bell commands the department from the engine room's starting platform, where the telegraphs from the bridge ring down their orders. His engineers are some of the best in the line. None of them will leave the ship.

OPERATIVE NOTES: A trimmer's or fireman's clothes, and a face black with coal dust, are the best cover in the ship. The engineers are officers and wear uniform. Orders arrive only by telegraph from the bridge; no one down here can see what the ship is doing.
