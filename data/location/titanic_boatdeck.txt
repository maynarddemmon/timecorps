BOAT DECK — RMS Titanic

The boat deck is the ship's topmost promenade, open to the sky, running most of her length between the bridge and the stern. First-class passengers take the air here, and the first-class entrance, the gymnasium and the officers' quarters open onto it. The four great funnels rise out of it.

Her lifeboats stand along both sides in Welin davits: fourteen wooden boats for 65 people each, two smaller emergency cutters, and four Engelhardt collapsibles with canvas sides, stowed flat. Together they hold 1,178 people. The davits were designed to carry many more boats than this. The empty space between them keeps the promenade clear.

No boat drill has been held for the passengers, and few of the crew know which boat they are assigned to.

OPERATIVE NOTES: The boat deck is open to first-class passengers and to crew on duty, and in the dark it is easy to move along unobserved. The boats are covered and stowed; lowering one takes a trained crew of several men and cannot be done quietly.
