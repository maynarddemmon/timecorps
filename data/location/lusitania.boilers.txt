BOILER ROOMS — RMS Lusitania

The Lusitania's twenty-five boilers are arranged in four boiler rooms running along the middle of the ship. Along her sides, between the boiler rooms and the hull, run long coal bunkers, meant both to feed the furnaces and to give extra protection to her machinery.

Since the war began, Boiler Room No. 4 has been shut down. The saving in coal and men helps Cunard keep her in service at a loss, but it costs her speed: with only three rooms fired she can make about 21 knots, not the 25 that made her famous.

The stokehold is a world of heat, coal dust and noise. The firemen and trimmers who work it are among the hardest-pressed men aboard, and wartime has made them harder to find.

OPERATIVE NOTES: Firemen's clothes and a coal-blackened face are almost perfect cover below. Relighting a cold boiler room is not quick: it needs men, coal trimmed into the bunkers, and hours to raise steam. Orders to do so would come from the chief engineer on the captain's word.
