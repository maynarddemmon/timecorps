RMS TITANIC — White Star Line, Southampton to New York

The Titanic is 882 feet long and measures more than 46,000 gross tons, the largest ship in the world by a small margin over her elder sister, the Olympic. Three propellers drive her: two outer screws turned by great four-cylinder reciprocating engines, and a centre screw turned by a low-pressure turbine that uses their exhaust steam. Twenty-nine boilers in six boiler rooms feed them. Of her four funnels, the fourth is a dummy, used for ventilation and added for looks.

Fifteen watertight bulkheads divide her hull into sixteen compartments, with doors that can be closed from the bridge. The press has called her practically unsinkable. Her builders never quite said so.

Her master is Captain Edward John Smith, the senior captain of the line, who is expected to retire after this voyage or the next. She carries more than 2,200 people across three classes, from the millionaires of her first-class suites to the emigrant families in third class near the bow and stern.

OPERATIVE NOTES: A ship this size is a small town, and its crew do not all know one another. A steward's white jacket or a fireman's rough clothes pass almost anywhere in their own department and draw looks in any other. Passenger classes are kept strictly apart by gates and stairways.
