BOAT DECK — RMS Lusitania

After the Titanic disaster, Cunard increased the Lusitania's lifeboats well beyond what the law had required. She now carries 22 wooden lifeboats in davits along both sides of her boat deck, and 26 collapsible boats stowed beneath them: 48 boats with room for more than 2,600 people, more than she is carrying on this crossing.

Having enough boats is not the same as being able to launch them. The davits must be worked by trained men, and many of her experienced seamen have gone to the Royal Navy. Her boat drills, held in harbour, involve a handful of men lowering one boat on the side away from the quay.

A ship that lists heavily makes launching boats very hard. On the high side the boats swing in against the hull. On the low side they swing out far from the deck. The collapsibles, stowed underneath, can only be reached once the boats above them are gone.

OPERATIVE NOTES: The boat deck is open to first-class passengers and crew. Boat drill is a matter for the chief officer and the deck department. A crew that has practised on every boat, on both sides, is a crew that can get people away.
