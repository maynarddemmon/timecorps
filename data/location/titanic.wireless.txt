MARCONI ROOM — Boat Deck, RMS Titanic

The wireless cabin lies just aft of the bridge, among the officers' quarters. It is a suite of three small rooms: the operating room with its desk and key, a sleeping cabin, and a sound-deadened "silent room" housing the powerful five-kilowatt spark transmitter, whose crackling discharge would otherwise drown out the incoming signals. The ship's call sign is MGY.

The set can reach some four hundred miles by day, and much farther at night. Its operators, Jack Phillips and Harold Bride, are employees of the Marconi Company, not the White Star Line. They keep watch around the clock between them, and their first duty is to paid traffic: passengers' telegrams, charged by the word and sent up from the purser's office by pneumatic tube.

Weather reports and ice warnings from other ships arrive here too. They carry no fee, and whether they reach the bridge depends on how busy the operators are.

OPERATIVE NOTES: The room is tiny, and a stranger inside it will be asked his business at once. The operators work alone for long stretches while the other sleeps. Messages bound for the bridge are carried by hand, and anyone in uniform can volunteer to take one.
